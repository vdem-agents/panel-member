# Annotated Bibliography

## CS Studies

**Xu, Rongwu, Zehan Qi, Zhijiang Guo, Cunxiang Wang, Hongru Wang, Yue Zhang, and Wei Xu. 2024. "Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey." *Proceedings of EMNLP 2024*, 8541–8565.**

Surveys how large language models behave when their parametric knowledge (learned during pretraining) conflicts with contextual knowledge (supplied at inference time), organizing the literature into three conflict types: context-memory (the case relevant here — provided text contradicts what the model already "knows"), inter-context (conflicting passages within the provided text itself), and intra-memory (the model gives inconsistent answers to differently phrased queries about the same fact, indicating internally conflicting parametric knowledge). Reviews causes of each conflict type, documents how models behave when conflicts arise, and surveys mitigation strategies. Establishes "parametric vs. contextual knowledge" as the standard vocabulary for this question in the NLP literature — a more precise framing than "anchoring bias" for what this study's B1–B4 and R4 hypotheses test, since context-memory conflict is specifically about reliance on stored subject-knowledge over supplied evidence, not the Kahneman-style numeric-priming effect that "anchoring" strictly denotes.

**Longpre, Shayne, Kartik Perisetla, Anthony Chen, Nikhil Ramesh, Chris DuBois, and Sameer Singh. 2021. "Entity-Based Knowledge Conflicts in Question Answering." *Proceedings of EMNLP 2021*, 7052–7063.**

Originates the entity-substitution paradigm for studying knowledge conflicts: named entities in a QA passage (e.g., person, organization, or location mentions) are swapped for other entities of the same type, creating a passage whose content contradicts the model's parametric knowledge about the original entity. Finds that models substantially over-rely on memorized (parametric) information even when the passage in front of them says otherwise — the mechanism behind hallucination in knowledge-dependent tasks — and identifies factors that make over-reliance worse (e.g., higher entity popularity, higher model confidence in the parametric answer). Proposes a training-time mitigation that reduces over-reliance on parametric knowledge and improves out-of-distribution generalization by 4–7%. The entity-substitution method is structurally identical to this study's name-swap test (R4): swap the named country between two evidence packets and check whether the rating follows the name or the text.
