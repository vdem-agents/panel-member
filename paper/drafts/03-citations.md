# §3 V-Dem coder panels — citations


### The V-Dem project

**Little & Meng 2024.** "Measuring Democratic Backsliding." *PS: Political Science & Politics*
57(2):149–161. doi:10.1017/S104909652300063X. Key `little2024`.
PDF: `_literature/Little and Meng 2024 - Measuring Democratic Backsliding.pdf`

> Criticizes V-Dem's reliance on expert judgment on the grounds that subjective ratings track biased coder perceptions rather than actual regime change. 

**Miller 2024.** "How Little and Meng's Objective Approach Fails in Democracies."
*PS: Political Science & Politics* 57(2):202–207. doi:10.1017/S1049096523001063. Key `miller2024`.
PDF: `_literature/Miller 2024 - How Little and Meng's Objective Approach Fails in Democracies.pdf`

>Responds to Little and Meng by arguing that backsliding occurs inside of democratic regimes and therefore cannot be measured by observables that only track levels of electoral democracy. Objective measures can usually separate democracies from non-democracies but cannot detect backsliding, which is subtle and often within the law. 

> The question of whether an expert's rating is based more on the details of the country case or the rater's subjective prior is not easily testable; however, for a synthetic coder the manipulation is relatively straightforward. 

### Measurement model

**Pemstein, Marquardt, Tzelgov, Wang, Medzihorsky, Krusell, Miri & von Römer 2025.**
"The V-Dem Measurement Model: Latent Variable Analysis for Cross-National and Cross-Temporal
Expert-Coded Data." *V-Dem Working Paper Series* 2025:21. Key `pemstein2025`.

> An ordinal item response theory model that estimates the latent country-year score jointly with two coder-level parameters: reliability `β_r`, which weights how much a coder contributes, and thresholds `γ_{r,k}`, which capture where that coder places the cut points between ordinal categories. This is what V-Dem publishes from — the raw panel mean is not what the project reports.


### Coder reliability

**Marquardt, Pemstein, Seim & Wang 2019.** "What makes experts reliable? Expert reliability and
the estimation of latent traits." *Research & Politics* 6(4):1–8. doi:10.1177/2053168019879561.
Key `marquardt2019`. PDF: `_literature/Marquardt et. al. 2019 - What Makes Experts Reliable.pdf`

> Takes coder-level data for six randomly selected V-Dem v8 variables, aggregates them with an ordinal IRT model that parameterises expert reliability, then regresses the resulting reliability estimates on expert demographic characteristics and measures of their coding behaviour. Finds little evidence of a consistent substantial relationship between most expert characteristics and reliability, including potentially problematic sources of bias such as gender. The exceptions: attentive and confident experts with contextual knowledge tend to be more reliable. Concludes that IRT models are a relatively safe method for aggregating expert-coded data.
